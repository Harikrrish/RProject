Bedroom planning: wardrobes, lighting and a calmer layout

A bedroom should work just as well on a busy weekday as it looks in a photograph. Start with the bed, the wardrobe and the space between them. The decorative details can follow.

By Alankaar Interiors3 min read

Place the bed using its full dimensions

Use the size of the bed frame, not just the mattress, when checking the layout. Add bedside furniture, door swings and the space needed to reach the window. If the bed has storage, check whether drawers can pull out or the lifting mechanism can be used comfortably.

Keep the people sharing the room in mind. Someone who gets up earlier needs a route that does not require stepping around a chair or squeezing past a table. Test the position before committing to wall panels or fixed bedside units.

Plan the wardrobe from the inside out

Count what you need to store: hanging clothes, folded clothes, bags, bedding and everyday accessories. Show the designer longer garments and bulky items rather than assuming a standard shelf arrangement will suit them.

Compare hinged and sliding shutters in the actual room. Hinged doors need opening space; sliding doors change how much of the wardrobe is accessible at once. Try a sample mechanism and check how internal drawers work with the shutters open.

Choose lighting for the things you do

Think separately about getting dressed, reading and winding down. A single ceiling fitting may not put light where you need it. Check the position of bedside lights against the headboard and the way you sit in bed.

Decide where switches and charging points should be before fixing the bedside layout. If two people read at different times, independently controlled lamps are worth discussing. Look at actual light samples alongside the room finishes instead of choosing from a screen alone.

Leave windows and services accessible

Keep the wardrobe, curtains and bed clear of openings you need to use. Show the designer the air-conditioner location and ask the installer to confirm service access and drainage requirements. A concealed detail should not make routine maintenance difficult.

Test curtains for privacy and daylight at the window. Fabric can look very different when light shines through it. Confirm the cleaning instructions before choosing a material that will be difficult for your household to maintain.

Finish with a few details you enjoy

You do not need a feature on every wall. A headboard, a bedside light or a piece of art can give the room character while keeping the rest simple. If you already own a bed or dresser you like, build the plan around it.

Before approval, ask for a bedroom drawing with the bed, wardrobe interior, switch locations and all opening clearances shown together. Bring your room dimensions to Alankaar Interiors to discuss an arrangement that suits your routines.
